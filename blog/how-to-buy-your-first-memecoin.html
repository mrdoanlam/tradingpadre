How to Buy Your First Memecoin: A Walkthrough

6 min read·Trading Padre Blog

Buying your first memecoin involves fewer steps than people expect, and more small decisions that matter than the "just connect and buy" framing suggests. Here's the actual sequence.

Step 1 — Get a wallet with funds on the right chain

Most memecoin activity happens on Solana, so a Solana wallet (Phantom or Solflare) is the common starting point. You'll need SOL for both the trade itself and network fees — keep a small buffer beyond your intended trade size specifically for gas.

Step 2 — Connect to a trading terminal, not a swap widget

A basic swap interface works for established tokens but lacks the discovery and safety tooling that matters for anything new. A terminal like Padre Terminal surfaces liquidity depth, holder concentration, and contract flags directly on the token screen — the same checks covered below, without needing a separate tool for each one.

Step 3 — Run the basic checks before you buy

  • Liquidity depth — is there enough in the pool for your trade size to matter, and for you to exit later without severe slippage?
  • Holder concentration — how much supply sits in the top handful of non-pool wallets?
  • LP lock status — is liquidity locked, or can the deployer pull it at will?

This takes two or three minutes once you know what to look for — see our chart-reading guide for how to interpret what you're seeing alongside these checks.

Step 4 — Size small on your first trade

Not because the mechanics are risky — because confirming your wallet, network selection, and slippage settings all work correctly matters more on trade one than optimizing entry price does. A small first trade is a systems check, not a real position.

A COMMON FIRST MISTAKE

Sending exactly your intended trade amount to your wallet, with nothing left over for gas. Keep a small buffer of the native token separate from what you plan to trade with.

Step 5 — Decide your exit before you need to

Set a stop loss and take profit at entry rather than deciding in the moment. Terminals like Padre support this directly on the order screen — it's the difference between a plan and a reaction.
