Reading a Memecoin Chart: What Candles Tell You

6 min read·Trading Padre Blog

A candlestick chart looks the same whether you're trading a blue-chip stock or a memecoin two hours old — but what the candles actually tell you differs enough that reading one the same way for both leads to bad conclusions.

What a single candle shows

Each candle covers a fixed time window and shows four prices: open, close, high, and low. A green (or up-colored) candle closed higher than it opened; red closed lower. That part's universal. What changes with memecoins is how much weight any single candle deserves.

Why memecoin candles lie more than you'd expect

On thin liquidity, a handful of trades can produce a candle that looks dramatic but represents very little actual volume. A single wallet buying a modest amount can print a large green candle on a token with a small enough pool — something that would barely register on a liquid asset.

THE FIX: LOOK AT VOLUME ALONGSIDE PRICE

A price move on high volume reflects broad participation. The same move on thin volume might be one or two wallets. Most terminals, including Padre Terminal, show volume directly alongside the chart — check it before reacting to a candle.

Reading the bonding curve stage differently

Pre-migration tokens (still on a bonding curve) follow a different price curve shape entirely — price moves mechanically with each buy/sell against the curve, not against typical order-book dynamics. A chart pattern that means something specific on an established pair often means something different, or nothing at all, at this stage. Our memecoin basics guide covers this stage in more detail.

Patterns worth paying attention to

  • Sustained volume on the way up — a price rise accompanied by consistently high volume suggests broader participation, not just one or two large trades.
  • A sharp spike with no follow-through — a single large green candle followed by immediate flatlining or reversal often signals one wallet's activity, not a trend.
  • Liquidity depth relative to your position — more useful than the chart itself is checking whether the pool can actually absorb your exit at a similar price to your entry.

What charts can't tell you

No amount of chart reading substitutes for checking holder concentration and contract permissions before trading — see our red flags guide. A clean-looking chart on a token with 80% of supply in one wallet is still a token with 80% of supply in one wallet.
