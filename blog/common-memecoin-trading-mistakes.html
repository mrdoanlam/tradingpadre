Common Memecoin Trading Mistakes (And How to Fix Them)

6 min read·Trading Padre Blog

Almost every memecoin trader loses money the same handful of ways before they lose it any other way. None of these mistakes require bad luck — they're habits, which means they're fixable with the right setup, not just willpower.

Sizing based on excitement instead of a plan

The trade that gets the biggest position is too often the one that feels the most exciting in the moment, not the one that actually clears the most checks. Deciding a standard position size before you start looking at tokens — and sticking to it regardless of how a specific setup feels — removes this failure mode almost entirely.

No exit plan until the position is already moving

Deciding a stop loss after a position has dropped 20% is deciding under pressure, with money already on the line — a worse position to make good decisions from than before you entered. Setting stop loss and take-profit levels at entry, which Padre Terminal supports as standing orders, means the plan you made with a clear head is what actually executes.

Trading the same size after a loss to "make it back"

This is the single most common way a manageable loss becomes an unmanageable one. A trade placed specifically to recover a previous loss — rather than because it independently clears your usual criteria — deserves a hard look before you place it, not after.

Skipping the safety check because it feels slow

Checking liquidity, holder concentration, and contract permissions takes about two minutes — see our full checklist. Skipping it to move faster is rarely actually about speed; it's usually about not wanting to find a reason to skip a trade you already want to make.

Treating every session as requiring a trade

Scanning New Pairs or Trending for twenty minutes doesn't obligate you to trade something. Most scanning sessions should end with no trade — that's the discovery process working correctly, not a wasted session.

Each of these is easier to avoid with a decision made in advance than a decision made in the moment. Presets, standing exit orders, and a fixed position size aren't just convenience features — they're what turns a good plan into what actually happens.
